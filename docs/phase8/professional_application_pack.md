## 6. Extended application pitch

A major project I developed focused on creating an auditable machine-learning and optimisation workflow for synthetic reduced-order inductively coupled plasma analysis. I treated it as both a data-science problem and a software-engineering problem.

The workflow begins with a reproducible synthetic monitoring environment, then uses persisted surrogate models for plasma-state approximation, a leakage-safe monitoring and ambiguity-aware diagnosis layer, and constrained operating-point optimisation. The optimiser operates only within a frozen domain of 15-90 W absorbed power and 10-60 mTorr, with Differential Evolution as the primary method and a deterministic 15,251-point grid retained as the reference and fallback.

I then integrated the trusted monitoring and optimisation interfaces through a deterministic agentic decision-support layer. The system fails closed when trusted boundaries fail, does not invent missing fault-to-optimisation mappings, and requires explicit human approval before recommendations could be acted upon.

The project formally closed with 793 / 793 tests passing. I also retained limitations transparently: although the surrogate models were highly accurate on locked synthetic TEST data, monitoring performance was weaker, including active-fault recall of approximately 0.67819. That contrast was important because it forced the architecture to reflect actual evidence rather than treating every subsystem as equally reliable.

The project demonstrates experience in Python, machine learning, numerical optimisation, model evaluation, persistence, testing, reproducibility, Git-based engineering and technical governance.

**What problem did you solve?**

I built an auditable workflow that connects synthetic plasma modelling, machine-learning surrogates, process monitoring, fault diagnosis, constrained optimisation and deterministic human-approved decision support.

**What was technically difficult?**

The difficult part was not simply achieving strong predictive results. It was maintaining trustworthy boundaries between modelling, locked evaluation, optimisation and decision authority so that later stages could not silently rewrite earlier evidence.

**What result are you most proud of?**

The final system reached 793 / 793 passing tests and preserved a complete formal evidence chain from model evaluation through runtime orchestration. The surrogate models also achieved very strong locked synthetic TEST accuracy.

**What did not perform perfectly?**

The monitoring subsystem had important limitations. Active-fault recall remained approximately 0.67819 and pressure-path diagnosis was the weakest final class. I kept those results frozen rather than retuning after locked evaluation.

**What engineering judgement did you demonstrate?**

I used ambiguity-aware diagnosis where mechanisms were not sufficiently identifiable, kept a deterministic reference grid alongside continuous optimisation, used fail-closed interfaces, and separated human approval from the authoritative scientific record.

**Why is the project relevant to data and analytics roles?**

It demonstrates end-to-end analytical work: data generation and validation, feature pipelines, model evaluation, numerical optimisation, reproducible inference, automated tests, stakeholder-friendly evidence and disciplined interpretation of limitations.
